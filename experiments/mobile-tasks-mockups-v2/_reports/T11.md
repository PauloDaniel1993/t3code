# T11 - narrow browser re-verification of D1

## Overall verdict

**PASS.** OpenSpec 6.2 passes at 320 x 844 in both themes, and OpenSpec 6.1 is
**IDENTICAL** to T9 at 390 x 844. D1 is fixed: every phone starts at x = 24 at
320 px, every phone's full 390 px frame lies in the document's reachable
horizontal scroll area, and the formerly clipped leading content is rendered.

I used local headless Google Chrome 150.0.7871.187 over the original `file://`
URLs. Native headless scrollbars were left enabled so the 390 px measurements
use the same geometry as T9. No server, build, or network source was used. The
checks used rendered bounding boxes and text ranges, actual end-of-scroll
positions, CSSOM inspection, browser console/page-error events, request-failure
events, and a real click-through navigation.

The expected boundary jump is visible: phone x changes from 24 at 389 px to

Adversarial caveat: if "no part of the phone" is interpreted literally to
include the outer bezel, the sampled 390 and 391 px widths retain 3 and 2.5 px
of negative bezel respectively. At 390 this is exactly the T9 geometry the fix
was explicitly required to preserve, so it is not a new regression. The
screen starts at positive x (6 or 6.5) and no content is unreachable.

## Vertical-axis finding - PASS, no new defect

I repeated all five pages in both themes at 320 x 600 and 390 x 600. Native
horizontal scrollbars make the scrolling element's `clientHeight = 594`. The
phone correctly used its 620 px minimum height.

- At both widths, every first phone began at y = 44 and every first screen at
  y = 53. No phone had a negative top edge.
- `chrome.html` placed its phones at y = 44 and 827.
- `state-matrix` placed its seven phones at y = 44, 809, 1574, 2373.5, 3209,
  4008.5, and 4790.
- Computed `align-items` remained `center` in every run. The positive y = 44
  start and ordinary document vertical scroll make all content reachable.

The horizontal overflow-splitting defect therefore has no vertical analogue
at the requested short viewport.

## Regression spot-checks

- The shared stylesheet loaded from its expected `file://` URL on every page,
  and CSSOM access succeeded without a parse/security error.
- Chrome reports **188 shared CSS rules**. This is T9's measured 187 plus the
  newly added media grouping rule. The separate figure of 197 mentioned in
  the brief was not reproduced by Chrome 150's CSSOM; the stylesheet still
  loaded and the new rule demonstrably applied below 390 px.
- Across 95 rendered document loads (the requested page/theme/width sweeps,
  both a4 renders, and the three-document click flow), there were zero console
  errors, zero uncaught page errors, and zero failed requests.
- There were no HTTP(S) or other external requests; all resources stayed on
  `file://`/data URLs.
- The real light-theme click path passed end to end:
  `flow-1-list?theme=light` -> click the login row ->
  `flow-2-peek?task=login&theme=light` with title
  `the t3 connect is not letting log in` -> click `Open thread` ->
  `flow-3-task?task=login&theme=light`, with the login task visible and the
  same title. The pinned theme remained `light` at every step.

## New findings

No new mockup defect was found. The bezel-only negative x at 390 and 391 px is
reported above because it is measurable, but the 390 px value is the explicit
unchanged baseline and the rendered screen/content stays fully reachable.
